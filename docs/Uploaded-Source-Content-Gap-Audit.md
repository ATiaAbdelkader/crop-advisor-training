# Uploaded-Source Content Gap Audit

**Scope:** All 31 retained document-derived modules (Modules 04–34), plus the three foundation modules that frame advisory practice.  
**Purpose:** Identify content that is absent or materially underdeveloped for a graduate who must work safely and credibly in the field.  
**Boundary:** This is an audit of the previously uploaded training files as preserved in the curriculum conversion record, module validation records, and source notes. The original PDFs are no longer retained in the working upload folder; therefore, the audit does **not** claim to recover text that is not preserved in those records. [1] [2]

## Overall judgement

The uploaded-file curriculum is **broad and technically coherent**. It covers the full vegetable-advisory chain: planning and crop fit; climate, topography, soil, nutrients, nurseries, establishment, water, irrigation, fertilisation, crop care, harvest handling, diagnosis, disease, pests, IPM, pesticide stewardship, and weeds. Every source-derived module has two lessons, six substantive teaching sections, an applied field brief, four scored assessment items, and an 80% threshold. [1]

The main remaining weakness is **not topical absence**. It is the gap between knowing a topic and repeatedly performing the connected field workflow: measure, record, calculate or compare, decide, communicate, verify, revise, and escalate. The uploaded sources contain many of these elements, but they are necessarily distributed across modules. A field-ready programme needs them reassembled into a smaller number of high-consequence operating routines.

> **Design conclusion:** Do not add unrelated subjects merely to make the course longer. Add source-grounded performance layers that force learners to use existing content together under realistic uncertainty.

## Priority source-grounded gaps

| Priority | Underdeveloped content layer | Evidence in uploaded-source curriculum | What learners still need to practise | Recommended content addition |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Integrated measurement-to-decision routines** | Soil sampling, soil context, water management, drip uniformity, nutrient planning and sprayer calibration all require evidence, but are taught in separate modules. [2] [3] | Select a measure, record units and conditions, compare it with a decision need, state uncertainty, and avoid a false precision claim. | Add six **Field Measurement and Decision Cards**: soil sample chain, root-zone moisture, drip uniformity, water-quality screen, nutrient-plan check, and sprayer pre-use/calibration check. Each must end with a stop/refer/review condition rather than a fixed rate. |
| 2 | **Quantified crop-health scouting and action thresholds** | Diagnosis, disease, pests, IPM and weeds require scouting, incidence, severity, patterns and review. [1] | Turn observations into a repeatable sample, count or compare, map hot spots, distinguish severity from incidence, and decide whether to monitor, act, test, or refer. | Add a cross-module **Scouting Protocol** with crop-stage sampling plan, observation sheet, incidence/severity scale, beneficial-organism field, uncertainty field, and action/review trigger. |
| 3 | **Water-quality, salinity and delivery-performance troubleshooting** | Soil degradation includes salinity; water management includes source safeguards; irrigation and drip modules include design, filtration, pressure and uniformity. [1] [3] | Diagnose the full water path—from source and water quality through filtration, pressure, wetting pattern, drainage and crop response—without assuming that more irrigation solves stress. | Add a **Water and Irrigation Performance Clinic** with three linked cases: salt or quality concern, non-uniform drip, and waterlogging/poor drainage. Use only source-supported checks and require laboratory or specialist referral when needed. |
| 4 | **Nursery and transplant quality assurance** | Nursery, bare-root, cellular, seedling-planning and transplanting modules contain quality, hygiene, hardening and establishment requirements. [1] | Run a batch acceptance/rejection decision, document sanitation and hardening evidence, calculate replacement needs, and investigate early stand loss. | Add a **Seedling-to-Stand QA Pack**: seed/lot and media record, germination and uniformity check, hardening checklist, transplant acceptance criteria, stand-count and replacement review. |
| 5 | **Crop-protection incident, hygiene and emergency workflow** | IPM and pesticide sources include storage, emergency first aid, hazard, PPE, environmental protection, weather, calibration and post-application review. [4] [5] | Stop unsafe work, secure an area, protect people and water, use the label/emergency source, report an incident or near miss, and document safe follow-up. | Add a non-product-specific **Pesticide and Crop-Protection Incident Drill**. It must assess stop-work, label lookup, PPE and hygiene, storage/segregation, water protection, emergency referral, and incident reporting—not brand or rate selection. |
| 6 | **Harvest-to-buyer traceability and rejection prevention** | Harvest and post-harvest content addresses maturity, damage, cooling, sorting, grading, sanitation and food safety. [1] | Connect harvest date, field/lot, maturity, handling, temperature or shade condition, buyer requirement, grading result, rejection, and corrective action. | Add a **Harvest Chain Record and Quality Review** with a crop-specific exercise chosen by the learner; include a buyer-quality specification and a post-harvest loss root-cause review. |
| 7 | **Enterprise decision revision under uncertainty** | Planning, cost planning, variety choice, climate, water, fertilisation and harvest content all address constraints and revision. [1] | Revise a plan when price, water availability, weather, labour, pest pressure, or buyer requirements change; identify what must be confirmed before spending. | Add an **Advisory Plan Revision Simulation** that combines a budget, crop calendar, water constraint, market condition and crop-health issue. Assess assumptions, trade-offs, communication and review—not predicted profit. |
| 8 | **Farmer communication as a teachable technical skill** | Planning is participatory, diagnosis stresses uncertainty, and field-readiness material adds communication and ethics. [1] [6] | Conduct a structured farmer interview, state a recommendation in plain language, explain uncertainty and costs, obtain consent for records, and agree a follow-up. | Add three short **Communication Rehearsals**: initial visit, difficult uncertainty/referral conversation, and post-visit review. Use a criterion rubric for clarity, consent, respect, safety and actionability. |

## Highest-value upgrade sequence

| Release | Additions | Why it should come first | Source modules it reinforces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Release A — advisory operating routines** | Field Measurement and Decision Cards; Scouting Protocol; Water and Irrigation Performance Clinic | These routines reduce unsafe or unsupported recommendations across soil, water, nutrition and crop protection. | 02, 03, 10, 13, 16, 23–26, 29–34 |
| **Release B — production quality systems** | Seedling-to-Stand QA Pack; Harvest Chain Record and Quality Review | These routines make crop establishment and market quality demonstrable rather than descriptive. | 17–22, 27–28 |
| **Release C — high-consequence stewardship** | Pesticide and Crop-Protection Incident Drill; Advisory Plan Revision Simulation | These routines prepare the learner for safety-critical and financially consequential uncertainty. | 04–06, 08, 23–26, 29–34 |
| **Release D — professional practice** | Communication Rehearsals and supervisor-reviewed evidence | This turns correct technical reasoning into advice a grower can understand, consent to, and use. | 01–06 and all practicum evidence |

## Content that should not be claimed from the uploaded files

The uploaded curriculum does **not** by itself justify detailed crop-specific pesticide rates, fixed Algerian regulatory conclusions, guaranteed market prices, product endorsements, laboratory-result prescriptions, emergency medical advice, or local crop calendars that are not currently verified. These should remain current-source, label, laboratory, extension, competent-authority, or specialist tasks. The course should train learners to **verify and refer**, not to memorise unstable facts. [4] [5] [6]

## Recommended graduate standard

A graduate should complete the current 80% knowledge pathway and additionally demonstrate, through the Field Readiness Portfolio, that they can conduct a safe field visit; use a measurement or scouting routine; write a bounded recommendation; communicate it to a grower; preserve traceable evidence; recognise an unsafe or uncertain situation; and review the outcome. This is the clearest route to a course that is more practical than competitors while staying faithful to the uploaded files.
