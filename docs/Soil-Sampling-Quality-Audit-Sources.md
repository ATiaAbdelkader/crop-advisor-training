# Soil-Sampling Quality Audit: Design Sources

The Module 16 quality audit teaches the sampling controls that make a laboratory result interpretable: separate unlike management zones, use clean equipment, collect a representative composite using a consistent method, preserve sample identity, and submit the sample with sufficient context for the laboratory.

| Source | Design implication used in the audit |
|---|---|
| [USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service, *Sampling Soils for Nutrient Management*](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/Sampling_Soils_for_Nutrient_Management_SD-FS-50.pdf) | The audit foregrounds uniform field areas, representative sampling, correct timing and handling, sample identification, and submission information. It does not import the publication’s local timing or depth settings as universal rules. |
| [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, *Standard operating handling and preparation of soil samples*](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/22390d4e-01e3-4188-a416-041beb9f918f/content) | The audit treats sample handling and preparation as quality-critical before chemical and physical analysis. |
| [University of Missouri Extension, *Soil Sampling Hayfields and Row Crops*](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g9217) | The design uses the extension principle of clean equipment and separate samples for distinct field conditions while requiring learners to verify their laboratory’s current instructions. |

The audit is an educational quality-control exercise. It does not establish local sampling depths, fertiliser rates, amendment decisions, contaminant conclusions, or laboratory interpretations. Those must follow the current receiving laboratory instructions and authorised local technical guidance.
