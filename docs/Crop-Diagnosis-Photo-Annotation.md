# Crop-Diagnosis Photo Annotation Exercise

**Author:** Manus AI  
**Implementation status:** Learner route available at `/diagnosis-annotation`

## Purpose

This voluntary, non-gating exercise gives learners a structured way to work with crop photographs without treating a photograph as a diagnosis. The learner places labelled evidence pins on a simulated field image, selects an evidence-led next step, and receives feedback on safe reasoning. It reinforces the programme’s existing standard that a credible crop-health conclusion compares affected and unaffected plants, retains field context, distinguishes evidence from inference, and preserves referral where uncertainty remains.

| Case | Visual-evidence focus | Required pin categories | Decision practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pattern before cause** | Uneven crop patch, healthier comparison plants, soil and irrigation context | Field pattern; affected/unaffected comparison; contributing condition; uncertainty | Compare zones and collect root-zone, irrigation, history, and plant-condition evidence before naming a cause. |
| **Describe before diagnosing** | Yellowing, lesions, healthy comparison foliage, missing context | Symptom/sign; affected/unaffected comparison; uncertainty | Record visible features, then examine plant part, distribution, signs, roots, water, and history before a cause or recommendation is made. |
| **Separate injury, pests, and beneficials** | Leaf injury, insect evidence, beneficial-organism evidence | Pest/beneficial evidence; symptom/sign; uncertainty | Record injury, pest stage or count, beneficials, crop context, and a repeatable recheck route rather than treating a sighting as a control decision. |

## Learner flow

The user selects a label, clicks a feature in the photograph to place a numbered pin, and can remove a misplaced pin from the image or annotation list. Completion requires every defined evidence category and one answer to each evidence question. The review screen reports the three safe decision choices and presents a recheck or referral boundary for each case. The exercise does not write learner data, does not change an assessment score, and does not alter the sequential module gate, 80% formal assessment standard, final certification, or owner-alert logic.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

The training images are clearly labelled as **simulated visual evidence**. They do not confirm a disease, pest density, nutrient status, water cause, threshold, treatment, product choice, rate, or legal action. The feedback therefore directs learners to compare zones and plant parts, retain sampling and beneficial evidence, use current qualified or authorised support when appropriate, and keep the diagnosis uncertain until competing explanations are checked.

> **Training rule:** A photograph can focus observation; it cannot replace field context, inspection, counting, verification, or qualified diagnosis.

## Programme connections

The route is directly available from the Field Readiness Portfolio and from relevant support cards in Modules 29–32: field diagnosis, disease management, insect pests and mites, and integrated pest management. It complements the Quantified Scouting Protocol and Printable Quantified Scouting Sheet; it does not replace the protocol’s repeatable sample route or the record required for a real field decision.

## Validation record

The regression suite confirms the three case identifiers, three generated visual asset paths, six annotation labels, mandatory uncertainty labels, safe answer availability, module mappings, meaningful alt text, and non-diagnostic boundary language. The implementation remains public and static so no learner crop, location, or observation data is stored.
