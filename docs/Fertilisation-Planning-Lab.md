# Fertilisation Planning Lab

**Status:** Implemented as voluntary interactive practice within Module 26, *Fertilisation of vegetables*.

## Purpose

The Fertilisation Planning Lab helps learners translate evidence already verified in a current crop guide, soil-test interpretation, product label, and local technical advice into a transparent planning draft. It is designed to reinforce the module’s limiting-factor principle and the 4Rs—right source, rate, time, and place—rather than to provide a universal fertiliser recipe.

| Learning step | Learner input or activity | System output |
|---|---|---|
| Establish context | Crop, variety, mapped field zone, area, and evidence note | A traceable decision context |
| Identify nutrient gap | Crop requirement and verified soil or other supply for N, P₂O₅, and K₂O | A non-negative nutrient gap in kg/ha and kg for the entered field area |
| Convert a label analysis | Product-label percentage for each nutrient basis | The independent product weight required to provide that nutrient gap alone |
| Draft timing | Locally appropriate growth-stage prompts, dates, 4R notes, and review triggers | A printable calendar draft that can be checked and revised |

## Calculation transparency

For a nutrient expressed on the same verified basis throughout the worksheet, the lab applies the following arithmetic:

> **Nutrient gap (kg/ha) = verified crop requirement − verified soil or other supply**
>
> **Product weight (kg for the entered field) = field nutrient gap ÷ (label analysis percentage ÷ 100)**

The nutrient gap cannot fall below zero. The lab withholds a product conversion when the area, input evidence, or product-label percentage is incomplete or invalid. It intentionally does not optimise a blend, adjust for unverified efficiencies, prescribe application concentrations, or infer a product choice from a crop name.

## Safety and learning boundaries

The learner must verify the nutrient basis, including the distinction between P₂O₅/K₂O and elemental units, against current local interpretation and the exact label. Before any field action, the draft directs the learner to check nutrient balance, placement, crop stage, root-zone moisture, weather, irrigation, loss risk, and local technical advice. Laboratory, extension, or qualified agronomy support remains the appropriate route for uncertain interpretations, unusual results, suspected interactions, or high-consequence rates.

The workspace is voluntary practice. It does **not** affect formal assessment scores, the 80% pass rule, sequential gates, competency scoring, certification, or owner alerts. The planning draft is not automatically saved as a field recommendation; learners may print it and, where appropriate, transfer verified actions into the existing private Fertilisation 4R Field Record.
