# Module 21–31 Standards Review

## Review purpose

This review evaluates Modules 21–31 as a connected production-to-protection sequence. The modules span field preparation, transplanting, water and irrigation systems, fertilisation, crop care, harvest handling, field diagnosis, disease management, pest and mite management, and integrated pest management.

| Criterion | Review evidence | Standard for acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Source grounding | Canonical lessons, applied field brief, assessment, and final competency | Content preserves the agricultural decision context, limits, and lawful safeguards in the uploaded source material. |
| Instructional depth | Two ordered lessons, outcomes, and explanatory sections | Learners receive both the technical concept and the field reasoning required to act safely. |
| Applied practice | Field-brief context, task, evidence, and quality standard | Each module defines a recordable farm action and the evidence needed to judge it. |
| Assessment alignment | Four scored questions, feedback, and semantic final-competency link | The module check tests a material field decision, while the final item addresses a corresponding source theme. |
| Accessibility and progression | Ordered lessons, 80% pass mark, feedback, and sequential gate | Learner requirements remain clear, usable, and appropriately sequenced. |
| Visual-support readiness | Textual field context and learner fallback behaviour | Instruction remains complete without image-dependent meaning; image generation remains outside this review scope. |

## Audit method

The review checks canonical course definitions, applied field briefs, and semantic assessment-alignment records for Modules 21–31. The absence of instructional images in this range is treated as an approved scope constraint: summaries, outcomes, field briefs, assessment feedback, and learner progression must provide complete instructional support without visual media.

## Findings

All eleven modules meet the reviewed content standard without requiring a curriculum change. Each has two ordered lessons with three explanatory sections, a four-question module check at the 80% pass mark, an applied field brief with a context, task, evidence record, and quality standard, plus a semantic source-theme connection to its related final-assessment competency.

| Module | Focus of review | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 21 — Field preparation, mulching, and trellising | Beds, drainage, mulch, and crop-timed support | **Meets standard.** The field brief turns uneven, poorly drained, weedy ground into a complete pre-establishment system decision. |
| 22 — Transplanting | Field and seedling readiness, careful placement, and stand recovery | **Meets standard.** The module prioritises hardening, moisture, crop timing, correct depth, and early loss checks. |
| 23 — Water management | Root-zone water-air balance, seasonal planning, and source safeguards | **Meets standard.** Learners must balance irrigation, conservation, drainage, and water quality rather than follow a fixed calendar. |
| 24 — Irrigation systems | Crop-stage demand, system comparison, and operational fit | **Meets standard.** The recommendation standard combines field evidence with labour, cost, water, slope, and maintenance capacity. |
| 25 — Drip irrigation system | System design, filtration, pressure, and uniformity | **Meets standard.** The applied brief traces non-uniform delivery across the complete water path before changing schedules or parts. |
| 26 — Fertilisation of vegetables | Limiting factors, source analysis, and 4R practice | **Meets standard.** The module rejects uniform-rate fertilisation and requires source, rate, timing, placement, and review to fit field evidence. |
| 27 — Field care and maintenance | Support, pruning, pollination, fruit set, and fruit protection | **Meets standard.** Structural crop care is treated as a connected, preventative system rather than isolated tasks. |
| 28 — Harvesting and post-harvest handling | Maturity, gentle handling, quality retention, and food safety | **Meets standard.** The quality standard protects produce through every field-to-market hand-off. |
| 29 — Field diagnosis in vegetable crops | Observation, elimination, uncertainty, and responsible recommendations | **Meets standard.** The module requires competing explanations and a safe, qualified next action before a high-risk intervention. |
| 30 — Disease identification and management | Diagnosis, look-alikes, spread pathways, prevention, and IPM | **Meets standard.** The product role is constrained by diagnosis, prevention, lawful use, and label direction. |
| 31 — Insect pests and mites identification and management | Pest stages, vectors, beneficial insects, and IPM | **Meets standard.** The field brief safeguards diagnosis, source removal, natural enemies, and target-aware tools. |

## Visual-support conclusion

Modules 21–31 do not currently have generated instructional images because image generation remains outside the approved scope. This does not reduce the reviewed instructional quality: text-based summaries, outcomes, field notes, applied field briefs, assessment feedback, and sequential progression provide complete learning support. If imagery is added later, it should follow the established Module 01–10 implementation: a source-aligned asset, specific alternative text, learning caption, and learner-safe fallback.

> **Review conclusion:** Modules 21–31 meet the source-grounded, adult-learning, applied-practice, assessment, competency, accessibility, and progression requirements. No content correction is required in this review scope.
