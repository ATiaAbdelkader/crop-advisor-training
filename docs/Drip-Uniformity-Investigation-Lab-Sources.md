# Drip-Uniformity Investigation Lab: Design Sources

The Module 25 investigation lab follows water from the source and filtration stage through distribution, emitter condition, wetting pattern, and crop response. Learners compare source-near, middle, and far-end evidence before changing a setting or proposing maintenance.

| Source | Design implication used in the lab |
|---|---|
| [Oregon State University Extension, *Inspecting and maintaining irrigation systems*](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9869-inspecting-maintaining-irrigation-systems) | The lab uses visual operating checks for leaks, fittings, filters or strainers, distribution components, and signs requiring maintenance attention. |
| [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, *Criteria and options for appropriate irrigation methods*](https://www.fao.org/4/w3094e/w3094e05.htm) | The lab treats routine emitter inspection and uniform wetting as essential evidence, while recognising that damaged or clogged components require context-specific action. |
| [Rutgers New Jersey Agricultural Experiment Station, *Drip Irrigation Inspection Checklist for Agriculture*](https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs1292/) | The design uses a concise, field-facing inspection checklist rather than an abstract systems lecture. |

The exercise does not specify a pressure, runtime, application rate, component design, repair procedure, or product. Learners must consult current manufacturer guidance and qualified irrigation support before changing a system or responding to source, electrical, pump, water-quality, or infrastructure concerns.
