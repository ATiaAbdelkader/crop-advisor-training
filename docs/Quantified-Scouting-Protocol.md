# Quantified Scouting Protocol

**Status:** Implemented as an open learner-support resource from Field Ready and Modules 29–32 and 34.  
**Purpose:** Build a consistent evidence sequence before a crop-health, pest, disease, IPM, or weed recommendation: plan a sample, interpret the pattern, count pressure and beneficial organisms, then decide and recheck.

## Professional boundary

The protocol teaches **comparable field evidence and review**, not universal treatment thresholds, product selection, pest confirmation, laboratory diagnosis, or legal authorisation. A percentage, count, severity class, map, trap observation, or 1 m × 1 m weed sample must remain connected to its sampling unit, crop stage, field context, and uncertainty. Current local thresholds, product labels, legal requirements, and specialist advice must be verified separately.

| Protocol stage | Reinforced source modules | Evidence to retain | Decision safeguard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Define the question and sample | 29–34 | Question, zone, route, sampling unit, sampled-unit count, crop stage | A convenient plant or edge is not a representative field conclusion. |
| Separate pattern, cause, and contributing conditions | 29, 30, 32, 34 | Affected/unaffected comparison, map, signs, symptoms, roots, history, water, weather, uncertainty | Pattern evidence is not a confirmed causal diagnosis. |
| Count pests and beneficial organisms | 31, 32 | Plant part, damaging stage, injury, pest count, beneficial count, trap context, host weeds and residues | Keep beneficial and pest evidence separate; a trap catch is not a whole-field density estimate. |
| Decide and review | 29, 30, 32, 34 | Evidence summary, rationale, constraints, baseline, recheck route/date, escalation trigger | Counts do not automatically justify control; verify current threshold, label/legal fit, people, water, crop stage, alternatives, and specialist advice. |

## Core evidence method

The first stage asks the learner to define the field question and use a repeatable route and sampling unit. The second stage uses affected and unaffected comparisons, signs and symptoms, roots where relevant, field pattern, water, crop history, and other contributing conditions to preserve uncertainty. The third stage separates pest stage, injury, density, beneficial organisms, and trap observations. The fourth stage turns the record into an evidence-led IPM, disease, weed, or diagnosis decision with a recheck using the same sampling frame.

> **Incidence** can be calculated as the affected sampled units divided by the total sampled units, expressed as a percentage when useful. It is not a standalone intervention threshold. **Severity** should be described with a defined, consistently applied field scale or damage class, not assumed to have one universal meaning.

## Module access and progression

The resource is linked from Field Diagnosis in Vegetable Crops, Disease Identification and Management, Insect Pests and Mites Identification and Management, Integrated Pest Management, Weed Management, and the Field Readiness Portfolio. Support cards remain visible while lessons are gated, but the protocol does not alter the existing module sequence, 80% checks, certificate rules, or owner alert.

## Validation standard

Regression coverage verifies the exact four-stage set, complete prepare/observe/quantify/record structure, valid module mappings, IPM/pest/weed entry points, explicit non-prescriptive decision boundaries, referral content, and safe unknown-stage fallback. Visual validation confirms the protocol workspace, five linked field-assessment modules, and the Field Ready entry point.
