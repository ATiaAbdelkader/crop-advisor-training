# Root-Zone Water and Air Decision Lab: Design Sources

The Module 23 decision lab teaches learners to compare root-zone evidence, irrigation and rainfall history, soil-air/drainage cues, crop stage, and field patterns before selecting a safe next check. It deliberately withholds irrigation durations, quantities, pressure changes, and universal crop thresholds.

| Source | Design implication used in the lab |
|---|---|
| [Oregon State University Extension, *Soil moisture monitoring to support irrigation scheduling*](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9868-soil-moisture-monitoring-support-irrigation-scheduling) | The exercise treats root-zone moisture observation as one input to water-management decisions and asks learners whether water reached the intended root depth. It allows field observation and monitoring methods without assuming that one method fits every soil or farm. |
| [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, *Crop evapotranspiration: Chapter 8—ETc under soil water stress conditions*](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm) | The exercise connects rainfall and irrigation additions, crop water uptake, root depth, soil water availability, and drainage. It uses these concepts for observation and comparison, not to calculate a local schedule. |
| [University of Minnesota Extension, *Irrigation strategies for vegetables*](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/irrigation-strategies-for-vegetables) | The exercise reinforces the need to consider weather and soil-water conditions rather than applying water on a fixed, unreviewed routine. |

The lab is an evidence and referral exercise. It does not replace water-quality testing, irrigation-system design, local weather information, authorised irrigation guidance, or specialist assessment of salinity, contamination, drainage, or infrastructure problems.
